# Paid buyer access recovery

A paid buyer reported missing access emails and a failed email-code screen. Production receipts show both access emails and a sign-in email accepted by the recipient mail server. The membership remained unclaimed. The sign-in route converted every error into a generic rate-limit response, and the form hid code entry after a failed resend. A shared-IP bucket had ten attempts against a limit of three. Checkout already collected a phone, but access texts only accepted separate webinar permission.

The access form now keeps code entry available after send errors, offers explicit resend guidance, and links to the correct iCloud/Gmail inbox when recognized. The broader shared-IP allowance no longer uses the much smaller per-recipient limit; per-recipient send and verification limits remain. Provider failures and rate limits are distinguished in responses and redacted logs.

New buyers can explicitly request a text code using their payment email and the phone saved on the settled purchase. It is eight digits, expires after ten minutes, stops after five incorrect attempts, and is bound to a random HTTP-only challenge cookie. Code delivery starts after the challenge cookie response, avoiding a provider timeout that strands an otherwise delivered code. Ambiguous sends are never automatically replayed. STOP, sender-role, payment, billing-review and transport settings are checked before sending and completing access.

Phone verification creates a private, revocable server session and claims only the proven membership. It does not mark the email verified or claim other orders sharing that email. Existing confirmed email accounts cannot be taken over by entering that email on a new checkout. An unclaimed purchase associated with an already confirmed email account must use email sign-in; a returning account can use phone sign-in only after this purchase-bound phone identity was verified. Email-specific actions retain their existing email-verification requirements. Account snapshot, bot setup, wallet ownership and membership/workspace gates are preserved. Session reads check identity revocation, expiry, changed phone/email and Auth deletion/bans; sign-out revokes the session.

New completed checkouts record the displayed transactional text notice and the canonical phone with the checkout binding. That receipt independently queues one access-link SMS even without webinar opt-in. No marketing enrollment, historical consent backfill, extra purchase or paid development message is created. Existing webhook, queue, STOP and duplicate controls remain.

Migration `20261017171814_paid_phone_access_recovery` is applied in production. Readback confirms private RLS tables, denied anonymous execution, service permissions and eligibility for the reported purchase. No phone identities or sessions were created by deployment. Security advisors reported no warning/error findings. Actual PostgreSQL and transport fixtures cover wrong contacts, expired/incorrect/reused codes, account takeover attempts, preserved setup, isolated claims, no false email verification, ambiguous responses, session revocation, cancellation support, checkout permission and duplicate protection. Browser coverage includes email failure followed by code entry and phone-code recovery on desktop/mobile. No real development OTP, SMS, email, call or payment was sent.
