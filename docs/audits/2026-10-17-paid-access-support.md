# Paid access and locked-out support

The paid-access release provided two purchase emails, a permission-bound access text, and email/checkout-phone sign-in. This audit found a support loop: the access page linked to support, which required the same unavailable sign-in. It also found no owner queue for unclaimed paid purchases and access-message delivery problems.

`/access/help` now accepts a help request without account authentication. A seven-day HTTP-only browser cookie holds a random secret; only its hash is stored. Customers can read and reply to their own conversation in that browser. This grants no account, payment, cancellation, or entitlement authority. Submitted email and phone remain explicitly unverified, and no payment details are exposed publicly. Codes, credentials and common sensitive data are redacted. Saved request IDs recover lost responses without duplicate questions or owner notifications. Origin checks, bounded inputs, private service-only database permissions, per-browser/IP limits, per-email case limits and daily message bounds constrain misuse.

The operator-only support desk shows these conversations, supports idempotent replies/status updates, and clears previously displayed private ticket data when authorization fails. Replies are visible on the buyer's help page without requiring email delivery. Existing signed-in support, cancellations and owner notification delivery remain intact. Sign-in and purchase emails link to this route, and purchase emails explain the phone-code alternative.

A live support queue includes active, settled, positive-value production purchases that have no linked account and either a delivery problem, an unconfirmed first email after five minutes, or no linked account after twenty minutes. Free/test/claimed/inactive purchases are excluded. A provider receipt never asserts successful account entry. Maintenance groups new cases into one owner digest at most every fifteen minutes, flags each purchase once, and leaves current status visible in the desk. Monitoring never resends a buyer message or grants access. Owner alerts use the existing verified-owner, idempotent, suppression-aware delivery queue.

The audit also found three active unclaimed paid purchases with no first access-email row. A separate production email-maintenance reconciliation queues only missing instructions for eligible purchases within thirty days. It excludes claimed, free, test, inactive, expired, billing-review and suppressed purchases, and never resets an existing send or uncertain attempt. Existing payment and permission checks govern dispatch; the normal second email waits fifteen minutes after the first provider acceptance, and a text requires the saved matching permission. Out-of-order delay webhooks cannot overwrite an already confirmed delivery, while actual failures, bounces and complaints retain precedence.

Focused checks execute the real PostgreSQL functions, public/operator routes and notification transport with fixtures. Browser checks cover the actual locked-out help link, failed-response draft recovery, a single saved request, owner reply, cleared data after denied access and reading the reply without signing in. Provider traffic is intercepted; no paid development email, text, code, call or payment is sent.
